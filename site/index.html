Meet Arbos

Every project gets an always-on agent on your own cloud machine. Message it or call it from your Mac or iPhone, and it comes back with the work finished.

Download for Mac Join the iPhone betaon TestFlight Sign in with GitHub

Message agents like teammates

Every agent is one conversation, the same on your Mac and your iPhone. Hand it work the way you would to a colleague; it keeps the context, messages the other agents it needs, and comes back when it needs your approval.

Work with many agents at once

Create an agent, give it a task, and add another when the work grows — one on the website, one on research, one on the books. They run in parallel on your machine, talk to each other when it helps, and keep working while your laptop is closed.

You stay in control

Anything that leaves the machine — an email, a Slack post, a payment — shows up as a draft first. Approve it, edit it, or say no. Logins and keys stay in an encrypted vault the agent never sees.

Give each agent a job

Clear your inbox overnight. Reads every thread through your Gmail connector, drafts replies in your voice, files what needs no answer, and leaves a short review list for the morning.

Research that keeps going. Follows a question across papers, docs, and your own notes, keeps a running memo, and pings you only when it finds something that changes the plan.

Ship the website. Edits the repo on its own machine, runs the build and Lighthouse, opens the pull request, and asks before anything goes live.

Keep the books current. Pulls receipts from email, matches them to transactions, drafts the monthly summary, and never pays anything without your approval.

Run the hiring loop. Screens applications against the brief, schedules through your calendar, and writes the debrief after each round.

Handle the trip. Finds the flights and hotel that fit your rules, holds them as drafts, books on approval, and drops the itinerary in the thread.

Watch production. A routine checks the dashboards every ten minutes, triages alerts, reproduces the bug on its machine, and wakes you only for the real ones.

Chief of staff. Sits in a group chat with your other agents, keeps the weekly plan, chases what is late, and writes the Friday summary.

FAQs

How is Arbos different from a chat assistant?

An Arbos agent has its own computer and its own memory. It can run code, use a browser, sign in to your tools, remember everything you told it, and keep working after you close the laptop. A chat assistant answers; an agent finishes the work and comes back.

Do projects share one computer?

Yes. Every user gets one cloud machine. Each project is a separate workspace on it with its own directory, process group, memory, and history. Isolation is per user, so your projects can hand files and context to each other, and nobody else's can.

Who runs the machine?

We do. The machine starts when you send a message and sleeps when you go quiet. Inference, hosting, and app updates are managed for you. If you would rather run it yourself, the whole stack is open source.

Where do I talk to Arbos?

From the Mac app, and from the iPhone app in the TestFlight beta. Each agent is one conversation that both devices share. You can also call an agent — voice or video, one-to-one or in a group with people — and pick the voice it answers with.

Can agents talk to each other?

Yes. An agent can message another agent directly — the hand-off shows in your thread as a short “Messaged Affine” line — and you can put several in one group chat. They pass work between themselves and you watch instead of relaying messages.

What can a project connect to?

Connectors for Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack, and GitHub, used as you. Routines that run on a schedule or on an event. A skills library you can edit. A credential vault for logins and keys. Channels so you can talk to it from Slack, Telegram or WhatsApp. Local access to your Mac, with a separate approval for every action.

How does Arbos handle my data?

Sign-in is GitHub OAuth. Tokens for your connectors stay in the relay and never enter the machine. Vault items are encrypted at rest and injected as placeholders, so the model sees ••••. Anything that leaves the machine goes through an approval card first. The code is public, so you can check all of this.
